## Mission Statement
The Iowa City Litter Crew empowers people to pick up litter in order to connect with nature and neighbors.

## About Us
Founded by Cara Keller in 2023, the Iowa City Litter Crew are volunteers who get together for monthly clean ups. We meet with business and community leaders to raise awareness of the need to manage litter and collaborate on practical solutions with relevant stakeholders.

## Our Goals for Change
- More trash cans in locations that have demonstrated high trash density.
- Litter reduction and storm sewer maintenance at the Whispering Meadows Wetland.
- Expansion of the Bottle Bill, to include deposit on non-carbonated beverages and an increase in the deposit.

## Take Action at Home
  - Use re-usable grocery bags
  - Reduce usage of single use plastics by taking your own cup for your drink to go and container for left overs when eating in. Request a mug when ordering for here.
  - Get a small pocket sized ash tray to carry with you, and dispose of cigarette butts in a trash can.
  - Support businesses that are clearly making an effort to reduce their use of single use plastics and maintain a cleaner property over those that do not.
  - Pick up litter while walking. It may feel odd at first, but don't worry, all the cool people are doing it. I can usually fill up a grocery bag during a dog walk.
  - Take your redeemable containers to the Can Shed rather than recycling. This supports local jobs, and you can donate your redemption funds to a local cause. (https://canshed.com/)
  - When at home or picking up litter, make sure to properly dispose of these items: 
    Vapes and small electronics, including rechargeables: Collect your vapes and small electronics (ie electric toothbrush) and take them to the hazardous waste center at the dump. (https://www.icgov.org/Home/Components/News/News/2630/390)

    Batteries: Ace Hardware accepts batteries.

    Why? To prevent landfill fires.
    (https://www.axios.com/local/des-moines/2026/05/20/massive-landfill-fire-erupts-in-des-moines-hours-after-city-warning)


## Getting Started with Open Litter Map
Open Litter Map is a tool that is developed and maintained by volunteers in other parts of the world. It is the tool we use to collect litter data while picking up litter. Collecting this data allows us to show and demonstrate litter patterns in terms of type of litter, litter behavior, and litter density. 

To sign up, go to [openlittermap.com](https://openlittermap.com/signup). Create your account. You can join our team by clicking on the Menu dropdown and select Teams. Select Join a Team. To join our team, Iowa City Litter Crew, enter the code 'CleanIC'. Now, when you take litter photos, upload and tag them, they will be in our dataset and reflected in our data. Make sure to review your account settings.

Here is a good tutorial of how to tag litter:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b8DwY3W9UEA


A few things to keep in mind:
  - Your first 100 tagged photos will need to be verified before they show up on the global map at openlittermap.com. 
  - Make sure you have your location services settings for your camera set to allow precise location so that the location of the litter photographed is captured. If there is not location data attached to the photo it will not be uploaded to open litter map.

Please feel free to email the litter crew or join us on a cleanup if you want to contribute to our litter data and are running into walls using the app. One of us will gladly assist.

Litter is a reflection of our values. It reflects our decisions in terms of how we prioritize resources, and whether we choose to pay the cost to reduce litter or do we choose to externalize the cost to our wildlife, our soil and water, or downstream communities. For example, do we spend money on a trash can and maintenance in a fishing area, or do we externalize that cost and allow litter to be carried downstream and impact wildlife and downstream communities? Do we choose to refuse to provide trash services to the unhoused population, resulting in unsightly litter hotspots, or do we choose to provide a trash service? Do we spend choose to use re-usable cups or do we choose single use plastic cups and create a waste stream cost for tax payers and a cost to our environment? Our litter data can give us a clearer view of the impact these decisions have and where we can do better.
