Our Purpose

Mission Statement

The Iowa City Litter Crew empowers people to pick up litter in order to connect with nature and neighbors.

Who We Are

About Us

Founded by Cara Keller in 2023, the Iowa City Litter Crew are volunteers who get together for monthly cleanups. We meet with business and community leaders to raise awareness of the need to manage litter and collaborate on practical solutions with relevant stakeholders.

Monthly Cleanups

We organize neighborhood litter pickups across Iowa City every month — tackling parks, trails, commercial corridors, and residential streets.

Community Advocacy

We partner with local businesses, the City of Iowa City, and community organizations to address the root causes of litter in our neighborhoods.

Citizen Science

Volunteers photograph and tag litter using the OpenLitterMap app, building an open dataset that tracks pollution hotspots over time.

Youth Engagement

We believe in growing the next generation of environmental stewards, welcoming families, students, and youth groups to all our events.

Our Advocacy

Our Goals for Change

Beyond picking up litter, the Iowa City Litter Crew advocates for lasting change. Here are the concrete improvements we are working toward.

More Trash Cans

Install additional trash cans in locations that have demonstrated high litter density — making it easier for people to dispose of waste properly right where they are.

Whispering Meadows Wetland

Dedicated litter reduction and storm sewer maintenance at the Whispering Meadows Wetland, one of Iowa City's most litter-impacted natural areas.

Expand the Bottle Bill

Expand Iowa's bottle deposit law to include non-carbonated beverages such as water bottles and energy drinks, and increase the deposit amount to better incentivize recycling.

Why It Matters

Harmful Impacts

The litter we pick up in Iowa City isn't just unsightly — the materials themselves pose serious environmental and health risks.

Cigarette Butts

Cigarette filters are the most commonly littered plastic item globally. They leach nicotine and heavy metals into soil and waterways, and take up to 10 years to break down.

Read more →

Disposable Vapes

Single-use e-cigarettes contain lithium batteries, heavy metals, and plastic. Improper disposal creates fire hazards and toxic contamination — and their use is rapidly growing.

Read more →

Single-Use Cups

Most disposable coffee cups have a plastic lining that makes them impossible to recycle. Billions end up in landfills and waterways each year.

Read more →

Plastic Bags

Plastic bags clog storm drains, harm wildlife, and break into microplastics. An estimated 100 billion are used in the U.S. each year, with less than 1% recycled.

Read more →

Make a Difference

Take Action at Home

Reducing litter starts with everyday choices. Here are simple things you can do right now.

Use Reusable Grocery Bags

Keep a reusable bag in your car or backpack so you're always ready. Plastic bags are one of the most common items found as litter in our neighborhoods.

Bring Your Own Cup & Container

Take your own cup for drinks to go and a container for leftovers when eating in. Request a mug when ordering for here. Single-use cups and food packaging make up a large share of what we pick up.

Carry a Pocket Ashtray

Get a small pocket-sized ashtray to carry with you, and dispose of cigarette butts in a trash can. Cigarette filters are the most littered item we find across Iowa City.

Support Responsible Businesses

Choose businesses that are clearly making an effort to reduce single-use plastics and maintain a cleaner property — and let others know why you made that choice.

Pick Up Litter While Walking

It may feel odd at first, but don't worry — all the cool people are doing it. A grocery bag is usually enough to fill on a dog walk.

Redeem Cans at the Can Shed

Rather than recycling, take your redeemable containers to the Can Shed. It supports local jobs — and you can donate your redemption funds to a local cause.

Visit the Can Shed →

Dispose of Hazardous Items Properly

When at home or picking up litter, keep these out of regular trash to prevent landfill fires:

Vapes & small electronics (including rechargeables such as electric toothbrushes) — collect and take them to the hazardous waste center at the landfill.

Hazardous waste drop-off →

Batteries — Ace Hardware accepts them. Proper disposal prevents landfill fires.

Contribute to the Data

Getting Started with OpenLitterMap

OpenLitterMap is a tool developed and maintained by volunteers around the world. It's what we use to collect litter data while picking up — letting us track litter patterns by type, behavior, and density across Iowa City.

How to Join Our Team

  1. Go to openlittermap.com and create a free account.
  2. Open the menu and select Teams → Join a Team.
  3. Enter the code CleanIC to join the Iowa City Litter Crew team.
  4. When you upload and tag litter photos, they'll appear in our dataset and show up in our Data tab.

New to tagging? Watch this tutorial to see how it works.

Sign Up for OpenLitterMap

A Few Things to Keep in Mind

  • Your first 100 tagged photos will need to be verified before they appear on the global map.
  • Make sure your camera's location services are set to precise location — photos without location data cannot be uploaded.
  • Have questions or running into trouble with the app? Email us or join us on a cleanup — one of us will gladly help.
Email the Litter Crew

Litter is a reflection of our values — our decisions about how we prioritize resources, and whether we pay the cost to reduce litter or externalize it to our wildlife, soil, water, and downstream communities. Do we put a trash can near a fishing area, or let litter wash downstream? Do we provide trash services for the unhoused, or create litter hotspots? Do we choose reusable cups or single-use plastic? Our litter data gives us a clearer view of the impact these decisions have — and where we can do better.
